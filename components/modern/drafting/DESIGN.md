# Drafting instrument — Anatomy Figure

Concept: a small Cartesian pen plotter turns a technical drawing into a precise physical movement. The crossbeam moves along the two bed rails; its stylus follows the drawing on the paper. It illustrates the careers page's existing Technical Innovation theme, without representing the firm's process or promising a legal outcome.

One shared path model drives the stylus, the ink trail, and the travel readout. Pointer position and arrow keys select progress along that path; the idle tour uses the same input. Pausing, leaving the viewport, or hiding the tab stops the loop. Reduced motion gives a still drawing and immediate manual positioning.

Parts planned before geometry (Figure scale, 40–80 parts):

- Base: four rubber pads, four foot blocks, bevelled bed, inset paper support, paper sheet, four paper clamps, four clamp screw heads.
- Guides: two bed rails, four guide mounting pads, four guide end stops, eight rail screw heads, front ruler strip, its ticks and two fasteners.
- Carriage: four bearing shoes, two vertical brackets, crossbeam, crossbeam ruler strip, two end caps, four bracket fasteners.
- Stylus: moving mount block, clamp collar, square stylus cartridge, cap, shank, nib, two clamp fasteners.
- Drive and control: rear drive enclosure, inset cover, six closed cooling slots, control plate and two fasteners.

All structural pieces use filled, bevelled solids from the MIT-licensed Anatomy kit. The kit is vendored under tools/anatomy with its license; only generated SVG paths and the small shared motion model ship to the browser. No external scripts, machine runtime, or WebGL are required. Geometry audits and close-ups cover resting and moving poses.
